Ada Lovelace

Software Engineer

Ada Lovelace was a 19th-century mathematician often celebrated as the world's first computer programmer. As the daughter of the poet Lord Byron, she combined her father’s imagination with her mother’s logic, a blend she called "poetical science." She is most famous for her collaboration with Charles Babbage on his Analytical Engine, an early design for a general-purpose computer. In her extensive notes on the machine, she authored an algorithm to calculate Bernoulli numbers, recognized today as the first computer program. Crucially, Lovelace looked beyond mere math, realizing that machines could manipulate symbols to create music or art. Her visionary insight bridged the gap between calculation and modern computing. Though she died young at age 36, her legacy lives on through the Ada programming language and Ada Lovelace Day, which honors women in STEM.

Note G

Note G is the final and most famous of seven extensive appendices written by Ada Lovelace in 1843 to accompany her translation of an article on Charles Babbage's Analytical Engine. It is historically significant because it contains what is widely considered the first computer program ever published.
